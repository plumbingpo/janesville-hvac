Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Green Bay

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and schedule a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still cheap.

Age matters too. Most central air conditioners run 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15 with regular service. If your system is past that window and showing symptoms, such as climbing utility bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or repairs stacking up season after season, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and far less stressful than an emergency one in the middle of a cold snap.

Minor issues rarely stay small for long. Consider a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and just like that a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a miserable night.

Common HVAC Problems in Green Bay

Most local HVAC failures announce themselves before they happen. A breaker that trips more than once, a condenser that hums louder each month, rooms drifting further from the thermostat setting, energy bills creeping up with nothing else changed. Those are early signs of failing capacitors, refrigerant leaks, dirty coils, and duct problems, and caught early they're an affordable repair. Ignored, they take out compressors and heat exchangers, the two most expensive parts in the system. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so small problems stay small.
