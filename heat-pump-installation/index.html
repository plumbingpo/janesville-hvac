Many Janesville, WI homeowners replacing an aging furnace and AC are switching to a single heat pump instead, and the reason is efficiency. A heat pump does not burn fuel or heat a resistance coil; it moves heat. In cooling mode it carries heat out of the house, and in heating mode a reversing valve sends the refrigerant the other way so the compressor gathers heat from outdoor air and releases it indoors. That lets it deliver two to three times more heat than the electricity it consumes. Modern cold-climate equipment with inverter-driven compressors keeps working well below freezing, which the older units behind the technology's old reputation simply could not do.

Great heat pump performance starts with design work, and that is where Janesville HVAC earns your trust. A heat pump has to be sized for two loads, summer cooling and winter heating, and the design has to find the balance point where the outdoor temperature meets the unit's capacity curve. Our experienced technicians run the load calculation, compare single-stage, two-stage, and variable-speed inverter equipment against your climate and ductwork, and plan the backup heat properly, either electric strips staged below the balance point or a dual-fuel setup with a gas furnace that takes over in deep cold. We also check your electrical service and ducts, since heat pumps deliver lower supply temperatures than furnaces and need solid airflow to feel comfortable.

Installation follows the same discipline as every Janesville HVAC system: line set pressure-tested and evacuated to a micron-verified vacuum, charge weighed in and confirmed with superheat and subcooling, the outdoor unit elevated on a stand where winter defrost drainage demands it, condensate handled properly, and the thermostat configured so staging, backup lockouts, and defrost behave the way the engineering intended. Then we commission in both modes, heating and cooling, and record the readings. Maintenance Tips for Heat Pump Installation

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Replace filters on time, since restricted airflow hurts heat pump performance year-round
  • Watch the first winter bill; a sudden spike usually means backup heat is overworking
  • Avoid deep thermostat setbacks, which can call on costly backup heat during recovery
  • Schedule spring and fall maintenance to keep the charge and defrost checked
  • Clear snow, leaves, and plants away from the outdoor unit in every season
  • Rinse the outdoor coil each spring so summer performance starts clean
  • Light frost and an occasional defrost hiss are normal in winter, but solid ice calls for service
