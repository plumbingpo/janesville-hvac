Think about the spaces your central system has always struggled with: a garage workshop that bakes by July, an addition the ductwork never reached, a bonus room that runs too warm or too cold, or an older Janesville home built without ducts in the first place. Those are exactly the jobs a ductless mini split was made for. Nothing travels through leaky ductwork, because each wall-mounted head is fed by a line set that passes through a small hole in the wall. Inside the outdoor unit, an inverter compressor modulates to match the load, so the room holds within a fraction of a degree, quietly and on very little power. And when winter comes, it runs as a true cold-climate heat pump.

Design is where mini split projects are won or lost, and Janesville HVAC does it deliberately. Each zone gets sized to its actual load, because a head too big for the room cools fast but leaves the air clammy. Multi-zone systems bring their own engineering: the outdoor unit's capacity has to be matched carefully across the connected heads, line set lengths and elevation changes must stay inside the manufacturer's tables, and the choice between wall-mounted, ceiling-cassette, and floor units affects both comfort and how the room looks. Our experienced technicians walk the space with you, place heads where the physics and the sightlines agree, and route line sets cleanly, in finished line-hide outdoors.

Our installation process is held to the same standard as our biggest central systems. Nitrogen goes into the line set first to prove it holds pressure, then a vacuum pump pulls it down to a level confirmed with a micron gauge. Flares are torqued and leak-tested one by one, since that joint is where sloppy work shows up a season later. Drainage is set up with real fall or a reliable condensate pump, and the refrigerant charge is adjusted to match the line length. Finally, every zone is commissioned in both modes and we show you how the remote actually works. Why Professional Ductless Mini Split Installation Matters

Mini splits look simple hanging on the wall, and that illusion creates a steady stream of failed installs. The flare connections that join the line set are the system's weak point: under-torqued or badly formed flares weep refrigerant for months, and the first symptom is a head blowing room-temperature air a season later. Skipping the nitrogen pressure test and micron-verified vacuum leaves moisture in a circuit that inverter compressors tolerate poorly. Line sets run past the manufacturer's length and lift tables quietly cost capacity, and a condensate drain without proper fall leaves a water stain the first humid week.

Sizing discipline matters even more here than with central systems, because the smallest available heads are already generous for many bedrooms; the wrong pick short-cycles, leaves humidity behind, and never settles. Our experienced technicians size each zone honestly, torque and leak-test every flare, pressure-test and evacuate to a measured vacuum, set charge adjustments to the line length, and commission each head in both modes. Refrigerant work is federally regulated, and the electrical side needs a properly sized circuit and disconnect. The result is what a mini split should be: silent, efficient, and uneventful for fifteen years, instead of a mysterious underperformer from a rushed weekend install.

Ductless Mini Split Installation Prevention Tips

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Book annual professional service to verify charge, flares, and drainage while fixes are cheap
  • Let the inverter do its job: pick a temperature and leave it rather than toggling on and off
  • Schedule professional cleaning of the indoor coil and blower wheel before buildup turns into growth
  • Clear snow, leaves, and clippings away from the outdoor unit year-round
  • Pull and rinse the indoor filter screens monthly, because they fill up much faster than you would expect
  • On humid summer days, confirm the drain line outside is dripping as it should
  • In winter, expect defrost pauses and light steam from the outdoor unit; solid ice means call us
